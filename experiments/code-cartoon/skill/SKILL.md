# Cartoons made of code

A video is a program: every frame is a function of time `t`. You write the scene, HyperFrames opens
the page in headless Chrome, captures every frame and encodes an MP4. The sound is synthesized from
the same time constants. Everything runs locally; nothing is generated by a video model.

Talk to the user in their language and write on-screen text in the language they choose. These
instructions are in English only for maintainers.

User request: $ARGUMENTS

## Tools

All steps go through one command (it finds Python on every OS and pins the HyperFrames version):
`${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/cartoon <command>`. Below it is written as `cartoon`. Run project
commands from inside the project directory.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `cartoon new <dir> --format 9:16 --duration 12 --palette neon --title T [--template 2d\|3d\|photo3d] [--lang ru]` | new project; the starter scene works in every format |
| `cartoon new <dir> --template clip --format 16:9 --song s.mp3 --lyrics l.txt [--vocals v.wav \| --lrc s.lrc]` | a clip to a song: length from the song, parts + lyric lines laid out |
| `cartoon song [--lrc audio/song.lrc \| --auto]` | clip: parts of the song + lyric lines -> `cues.js`; `song.map.png`, `lyrics-preview.mp4` |
| `cartoon cut reel --auto --format 9:16` | clip: a cut (the chorus for Reels) as `cuts/reel/`, generated |
| `cartoon cut fight --from 41.8 --to 83.1 --size 1920x1080` | a draft of one part of a long video (staged review) |
| `cartoon example [name] [dir]` | list / build a bundled example (the best place to copy patterns from) |
| `cartoon lint`, `cartoon snapshot --at 0,3.9`, `cartoon render --output out.mp4` | HyperFrames, pinned |
| `cartoon check-hf` | HyperFrames checks of the page: lists `page_error` (a frozen frame = an exception) |
| `cartoon audio` | soundtrack from `cues.js`: effects + music mood + voice + your tracks -> `audio/mix.wav` |
| `cartoon voice [--fit]` | speak `voice` cues (Piper, the OS voice, a speech service, or your recordings) |
| `cartoon beats song.mp3 [--snap cues.js]` | tempo, beats, bars, energy, twist candidate; snap events to the grid |
| `cartoon check out.mp4 --duration L --fps 24 --size WxH` | delivery check: length, fps, size, loudness, first/last frame |
| `cartoon sheet out.mp4 -n 12` | contact sheet with time codes and the Reels interface zone |
| `cartoon frames out.mp4 --zoom X,Y,W,H --at ...` / `--twitch ...` / `--jumps --cues cues.js` | look closer: crops, twitching details, unplanned jumps and cuts that settle |
| `cartoon frames out.mp4 --around 117.6,124.5` | every drawing around each time (look at every cut this way) |
| `cartoon analyze ref.mp4 -o refs/analysis/ref1` | a reference video in numbers |
| `cartoon roto clip.mp4 . --name hero` | hybrid: video -> line drawings per frame |
| `cartoon photo . --photo p.jpg --image drawing.jpg [--face X,Y,R]` | prepare photo3d pictures |

## Workflow (do not skip steps)

### 0. Questions before any work
Ask in **batches of 3–4** with the question tool (or a list), 2–4 ready options each, the recommended
one first. Every batch has a **"STOP — decide yourself"** option: once chosen, ask nothing more and
take the recommended options. Never ask again what is answered.
Find out: the story (offer 2–3 ideas), style and palette (show `assets/themes.jpg`), format
(9:16 / 16:9 / 1:1 / 4:5), length, on-screen text and its language, sound (synthesized by default /
narration / silent / their music), references (3–5 videos in one manner), brand (logo/fonts/colours).
If the project has a CLAUDE.md or a brand book, take colours, fonts and logo from it.
If they bring a child's drawing or a photo: ask who is in it and what should happen to it, and plan
the `photo3d` template or the recipes in `references/recipes.md`.
If they want a clip to a song: ask for the song file, the lyrics with their tags as in Suno/Udio, the
vocal stem (Suno: Get Stems) or an LRC, the full clip and/or a cut for Reels, and the worlds for verse and
chorus — then follow `references/song-clip.md` (template `clip`). **Read the music first**: you
cannot hear it, so say that, run `cartoon song` and give a 3–5 sentence reading from its per-part table
(key, loudness, brightness, bass, density), then ask what numbers cannot tell: the genre and lead
instrument, the story the music tells, how it should end, what the cover means (often only palette and
mood). Build the story on their answers, not on the title, and check the storyboard against the reading
(a bright breakthrough over the darkest part, a victory over a minor fading end are contradictions). Treat photos of real people,
especially children, as private: never publish them anywhere, and never send them to a generative service
(`cartoon gen`): it sends prompts, pictures and audio to the user's account at that service. Use it only
when the user has set keys up, and say what is sent before sending the user's own material. Generating
anything (a picture, a voice, a video) is the user's call: say what for, the model, how many and the price
(free or paid) and wait for a yes; a fal call prints its price and stops until it is run with `--yes`
(`references/generate.md`).

### 1. Environment (once per machine)
`cartoon doctor`. Needs Node.js 22+, FFmpeg, Python 3 + numpy, scipy, Pillow. Say what you install and why
before installing. Details and workarounds: `references/hyperframes.md`.

### 2. References (if any)
`cartoon analyze ref.mp4 -o refs/analysis/ref1` for each, then **look** at `sheet.jpg` and the frames.
Write down 6–10 rules repeated in all examples (drawings per second, lines, fill or hatching, paper,
palette, transitions, camera, hit rhythm, sound). Take techniques, never copy frames.

### 3. Story -> storyboard (before any code)
Story: 3–6 sentences. Storyboard: the table `time | drawn | camera | sound` in `project.md`.
Rules (`references/storyboard.md`), in short:
- up to 15 s — one continuous scene in 3–4 phases; from 20 s — a chain of scenes, the last object of a
  scene becomes the first object of the next (several worlds: `Sketch.sequence`);
- **one twist** (click, hit, flash, dive) at 35–45 % of the length: before it quiet, small, cold;
  after it bright, big, warm; keep the `warm` accent for the payoff (a clip: the twist is the first chorus,
  the storyboard is by parts of the song, see `references/storyboard.md`);
- the last 3 s are a **poster**: all text written, nothing fast moves;
- **the first frame is not empty** (it is the autoplay cover): start drawing before 0 (negative `t`).
Show the story and storyboard to the user unless they chose STOP.

### 4. Project and scenes
`cartoon new ./my-video --format 9:16 --duration 12 --palette neon --title "TITLE"` (3D: `--template 3d`;
real photo + 3D: `--template photo3d`). Put the storyboard timing into `cues.js`, rewrite `scene.js`
(the starter shows the structure; `cartoon example` shows more). Iron rules (details in pitfalls):
1. Everything is a function of `t`; no `Math.random`, `Date.now`, `requestAnimationFrame`.
2. Randomness only `Sketch.rng(id, ...)`, keyed by the object, never a shared stream.
3. The time of every event is a constant in `cues.js`; the scene reads `d.cue("id")`, the sound reads the same.
4. Colours only by role (`"line"`, `"a1"`, `"warm"`): the video recolours with one `PALETTE` line.
5. Hand-drawn style: 12 drawings/s (on twos), lines redrawn every drawing (pass `k`), no motion blur.
6. Fills -> hatching. Hatch the light on dark paper, the shadow on light paper.
7. Text: capital height >= 48–56 px per 1080 px of width (Caveat >= 80 px, Inter >= 66 px), slogan >= 64 px;
   in 9:16 keep the bottom 15 % empty (Reels/Shorts interface).
8. Several worlds (pencil -> print, yard -> space): `Sketch.sequence` with one palette and camera per scene;
   never switch palettes by hand inside a cached drawing (pitfalls).
9. **Cuts without jerks.** A cut is one changed frame; the drawings right after it are as calm as any others.
   - One path and one camera per shot, chosen once per drawing. A path that switches its formula by time
     (`floor(t / phrase)`) jumps at the cut, and everything that looks at a window of time around `t`
     sees the jump: speed, bank, a lagging camera, trails.
   - The camera's subject comes from the plan of the shot, never from a condition such as "whoever is
     behind now". A condition flips at random moments, and every flip is an unplanned cut.
   - A shot ends while its action is in the frame; the next shot starts with what the last one ended on.
   - Planned cuts and flashes go into `cues.js` as events (`"type": "none"`, a flash with its `dur`).

### 5. Check the frames yourself before showing anything
`cartoon lint`, then `cartoon snapshot --at 0,<phases>,<twist-0.05>,<twist+0.1>,<poster>,<end>`.
Open `contact-sheet.jpg` and 2–3 frames at full size. For faces, eyes, small text and logos use
`cartoon frames out.mp4 --zoom X,Y,W,H --at ...` and `--twitch` on what must hold still.
Look at every camera cut drawing by drawing: `cartoon frames out.mp4 --around <cut times>`.
Check by `references/qa.md`: the story reads without sound; text is big and not covered; the first frame
is not empty, the last one is a poster; nothing jumps that should stand; the frame is not overloaded.
A frame frozen from some time on (or an empty world) means the scene threw an exception: run
`cartoon check-hf`, it lists `page_error`. Fix and shoot again. Do not show unchecked results.
Long videos (over ~30 s, clips, 3D) go to the user **in stages**: keyframes, then a draft of each part
(`cartoon cut <part> --from X --to Y --size WxH`, render inside `cuts/<part>`), then the full render.
Keep every version the user has seen beside the new one (`out-v2.mp4` + `versions/v2/` with the
sources); never overwrite it. For important videos give the sheet to a
separate critic agent that has not seen the code (only if the user agrees to spend the extra run).

### 6. Sound
`cartoon audio` after EVERY timing change in `cues.js`. Narration: `voice` cues with `text`, then
`cartoon voice --fit` and `cartoon audio` (music ducks under the voice). Their song: put it in `audio/`,
`cartoon beats audio/song.mp3 --snap cues.js` (look at the PNG map), then `cartoon audio`. A clip:
`cartoon song` already put the song in; check the lines in `lyrics-preview.mp4` before rendering (you
cannot listen: ask the user to watch it, or say the timing was checked on the map only).
Target −16…−14 LUFS, peak <= −1 dBFS.

### 7. Render and hand over
```bash
cartoon render --fps 24 --quality looks --output out.mp4
cartoon check out.mp4 --duration <L> --fps 24 --size 1080x1920
cartoon frames out.mp4 --jumps --cues cues.js
cartoon sheet out.mp4 -n 12
```
Look at every flag of `--jumps` with `--around` before calling it "just a camera cut". A cut that is
not in `cues.js` is unplanned, and "N big changes in 0.3 s" is a glitch.
A clip: render the cut first (`cartoon cut reel --auto --format 9:16`, then render inside `cuts/reel`),
the full song takes ~4 min per minute of video.
Look at `out.first.png`, `out.last.png` and the sheet. Hand over: the MP4 path, the story second by
second, the palette, what you hear, and **honestly what you did not check** (e.g. you did not listen
to the sound). `cartoon share out.mp4` makes a light copy for messengers. End with 2–3 options for
what next, plus STOP.

## A new mistake = a new line in pitfalls
Add every caught mistake (yours or the user's find) to `references/pitfalls.md` as
"symptom | cause | fix". Each video then costs fewer mistakes and fewer tokens.

## Limits (say them honestly)
No photorealism and no live facial animation: code only depicts. A real person appears through a real
photo (photo3d template), not through a 3D model of them. 3D by code means particles, geometry,
logos, materials, toy-like objects, pencil-drawn vehicles and clouds — not Pixar. Live motion comes through the hybrid (a filmed or
generated clip -> `cartoon roto` -> redrawn in style). Build long videos from 10–30 s scenes: a couple of
minutes is realistic, ten minutes in one go is not.
