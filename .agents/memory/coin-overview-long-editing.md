For Finance → Long Video → Coin Overview, use the user's approved pump-dynamic visual language by default: cinematic, high-contrast 16:9 scenes; layered, visibly animated graphics; topic-specific photos or illustrations where useful; and large, short, voice-timed captions with clear contrast. Before assembling a new long video, show the user the scene frames for approval. Keep the visual hold under 1.65 seconds where possible, preserve the approved script and voice timeline, and copy source audio without re-encoding for visual-only rerenders. Do not embed pseudo-text in generated images or invent labels, prices, or claims; compose any necessary title deliberately in code. Use the relevant coin's palette and imagery—HYPE's label and artwork are HYPE-specific, not a universal template. Validate the full render at two-second intervals. Deliver the finished MP4 in Telegram, using a verified private expiring link when it exceeds Telegram's upload limit.

For future videos, the user also approved a textured palette direction: graphite/black, terracotta, chocolate brown, cool pale blue, plum, powder pink, and deep cobalt, combined as deliberate gradients over subtle stone or plaster textures. Choose a harmonious subset for each subject rather than forcing every color into one frame. Treat the supplied screenshots as color/material references only; do not reproduce their social-media interface, account details, or controls.

**Why:** The user approved the PUMP reference and later approved the HYPE v4 adaptation, then asked for future long videos in the same style. The shared preference is the visual language and review workflow, not reuse of HYPE-specific branding. The frame approval step catches wrong layers, pacing, and composition before the more expensive full render.

**How to apply:** Restrict this treatment to long coin overviews; do not apply it to Shorts or other finance/video workflows. Build timed visual beats from the completed voice rows, use a clear lead/support caption hierarchy, keep output 16:9 at 30 fps, and review storyboard frames before assembly. For a new treatment, approve a 30-second pilot first; then inspect the finished long render at two-second intervals. Use the previously approved MP4 as the audio source for visual-only rerenders. When the textured palette is appropriate, implement it as background gradient/texture layers and keep captions high-contrast.
